Chapter 1Welcome to the training camp

Training with no enemy

Bacterial infection: the antibiotic finds its target.

Viral infection: no target — but our gut and skin bacteria still learn.

Most upper respiratory infections, sore throats and acute bronchitis are viral and self-limiting.
When the illness is viral, the antibiotic has nothing to hit — yet the patient's normal bacteria are still exposed. A training session with no enemy, but the recruits still learn.

Chapter 4Closing the camp

Antimicrobial stewardship: support, not control

Coordinated team support so every patient gets the right drug, dose, route and duration — and a timely review — for better outcomes, fewer side-effects and less resistance.

✅
Better outcomes for patients
🛡️
Fewer adverse events, including C. difficile
🔬
Antibiotics that still work when we need them
💰
Lower costs and better use of resources
WHO AMS training material · Barlam TF et al., IDSA/SHEA guideline, Clin Infect Dis 2016;62(10):e51–77
Stewardship is not someone checking up on prescribers. It's a team making the right choice the easy choice.

Chapter 4Closing the camp

The 48–72 hour review: the moment that matters

Cultures are back, the picture is clearer — one documented team decision:

STOPNot bacterial
SWITCHIV → oral
NARROWPer culture
CONTINUESet a stop date
OPATIV at home

Empirical broad-spectrum therapy is often right at the start — the review is what turns it into the right therapy.

UKHSA Start Smart – Then Focus · Nathwani D et al., Practical Guide to Antimicrobial Stewardship in Hospitals
Starting broad is often appropriate. The review is the step most easily lost in busy wards — and the one the whole team can protect.

Chapter 5One team, three roles

Each role holds a piece of the decision

🩺 Doctor
Owns the clinical decision
  • Indication and diagnosis
  • Empirical plan per national guideline
  • Documents indication & review date
  • Leads the 48–72 h review
👩‍⚕️ Nurse
Eyes at the bedside 24/7
  • Cultures before the first dose
  • First dose on time
  • Allergy: drug and reaction
  • Flags response, IV → oral, stop date at handover
💊 Pharmacist
The prescriber's safety partner
  • Reviews every antibiotic order
  • Dose for kidney & liver
  • Duration and stop date
  • Feedback on use & resistance

Closed-loop communication: no step lost at handover or discharge.

MOH Oman National AMS Policy (2025) · WHO AMS training material · Interprofessional antibiotic optimization framework
Responsibility is shared, not delegated. The doctor decides; the nurse and pharmacist make sure nothing is missed.
